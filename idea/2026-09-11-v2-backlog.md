# RouteFinder — v2 Backlog

Date: 2026-09-11 (revised 2026-09-29 for the eco-competition pivot)

Ideas deferred out of v1, captured in detail so they aren't lost.

**Revised 2026-09-29.** The product became a campus eco-commute competition — see
[`2026-09-29-eco-competition-vision.md`](./2026-09-29-eco-competition-vision.md). Some items below are
retired by that change, some survive it, and some are newly created by it. Each is marked.

## Motorcycle-taxi / driver availability — RETIRED

The original idea that kicked off this whole app: showing whether an informal motorcycle-taxi is currently available near you, not just the route to take.

Retired by the pivot. The product now exists to get people *out* of motorcycles and cars; shipping a
feature that makes catching a motorcycle-taxi easier works directly against the thing the app is
measuring. Recorded here because it's where the project started, not because it's queued.

## Public bicycle queue — STILL DEFERRED, now more relevant

Same shape of problem as bike/scooter-share systems generally: a bike sits at a station, gets checked out, gets returned somewhere (maybe a different station). Needs:
- `bike_stations` table (a station holds a *count* of available bikes/docks, not a single free/occupied flag).
- Check-out/check-in events tied to a user and a trip.
- Physical dependency: still needs bikes with some kind of lock/tracking, so hardware blocks the software.

The pivot strengthens the case rather than weakening it: a cycling leaderboard is worth more when
students who don't own a bike can still get one. Still blocked on hardware, so still deferred.

## Parking — DELETED

The 1-spot pilot worked: `parking_spots` and `parking_lots` tables, `GET /parking-lots` with
lot-level aggregation, and a rule treating a spot whose sensor had been quiet for 15 minutes as
`unknown` rather than free. It proved the sensor → backend → map pipeline end to end.

Deleted outright by the pivot, not merely retired: it was always blocked on sensor procurement nobody
has started, and helping people park works against an app built to move trips off vehicles. Recover
it from git history if it is ever wanted — it was working code, and the stale-sensor rule in
particular is worth reading before anyone rebuilds this.

Weather went the same way, for the same reason.

## The reward economy — SECOND VERSION

Points and the redemption catalogue are **kept in the codebase but held for a second version**. The
first version competes on distance and CO2; points accrue quietly in the background and the
catalogue still works behind the profile screen, but nothing in the pitch depends on them.

The reason to hold rather than cut: a leaderboard motivates the people near the top and nobody else.
If that turns out to be what happens — a hundred students on the boards and everyone else ignoring
them — a reward you can actually spend is the obvious second lever, and the plumbing is already
built and transactional.

What a real second version needs beyond what exists:
- Actual partner agreements (business development work, not engineering).
- A fulfillment mechanism per merchant (a redemption code, a voucher, an API integration — depends entirely on who the partner is).
- Probably a `merchants` table once there's more than one, so catalog items can be grouped/attributed.
- A decision on whether points and the boards should share a formula at all, or stay deliberately
  separate so that tuning one doesn't silently move the other.

## Crowdsourced building nicknames — STILL DEFERRED

v1 aliases (`place_aliases`) are admin-only. Opening this to students needs:
- A submission flow (probably just "suggest a nickname" on a building's page).
- Moderation before a suggested alias becomes searchable (to keep out junk/abuse) — admin approval queue, not auto-publish.

Unaffected by the pivot. Destination search still matters, and campus nicknames are still the thing
Google's place data doesn't have.

## Segments — CONSIDERED AND REJECTED

Strava's leaderboards are largely per-segment: a fixed stretch of path that many people cover, ranked
by time. It's the obvious thing to copy and it was rejected deliberately.

A segment is something you go and repeat for a better time. That is the looping behaviour the whole
design is built to exclude — the trip is supposed to be one you were making anyway. Adding segments
would reintroduce, as a headline feature, exactly the incentive that destination-based trips remove.

Recorded so it doesn't get re-proposed as an oversight.

## Social graph — DEFERRED (created by the pivot)

Following, friends, kudos, comments, clubs. The faculty is the only social unit in the first version;
the boards are the entire social surface. Deferred until there's evidence the faculty framing isn't
enough on its own. Would need a follow/friend graph and a per-trip visibility flag, neither of which
exists — every trip is currently private to its owner with no privacy model at all.

## Multiple universities — DEFERRED (created by the pivot)

The zone is one campus. `CAMPUS_BOUNDS` (`services/campus.ts`) and the single checked-in
`campusGraph.json` are hardcoded singletons with no tenant id, and every board is implicitly scoped to
"everyone in the database". Supporting a second university means a `universities` table, a per-user FK,
per-campus bounds, and a per-campus graph. Nothing is being built for it now; the point of recording it
is to avoid choices that make it impossible later.

## Machine learning — BLOCKED ON DATA, and more urgent than before

No ML anywhere yet, and it shouldn't be forced in early — every useful application here is cold-start blocked. You can't train on zero trips. The app has to ship and accumulate real usage first.

The schema already banks the training data for free: `trips` stores the full `gps_trace`, distance, duration, travel mode, and timestamps.

- **GPS spoofing detection** — the highest-value item on this list after the pivot. The current
  smoothness check (`isTraceSuspiciouslySmooth`, coefficient of variation < 0.05) is a hand-tuned guess.
  It was adequate when a faked trip stole points from nobody. Now a faked trip takes a place on a
  leaderboard and can decide which faculty wins, which makes spoofing worth someone's effort for the
  first time. A classifier trained on real traces vs. known-faked ones would be far more robust; the
  `flagged_review` status exists to accumulate the "suspicious" pile for exactly this.
- **Travel-time prediction** — "this route takes 12 min at 8am but 6 min at 2pm." Needs thousands of real trips across times of day before a model beats the static per-edge speeds in the graph.
- ~~Parking prediction~~ — retired with the parking feature.

The honest trigger for starting any of this is "we have enough real data to train on," not a version number.
